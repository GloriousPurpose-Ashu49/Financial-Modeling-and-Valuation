Amber Enterprises India Ltd. – Financial Modelling & Valuation
Project overview
This project analyses Amber Enterprises India Ltd. using historical financial statements, ratio analysis, a five-year forecast and valuation methods. The model covers actual performance from FY2020 to FY2025 and forecasts FY2026 to FY2030.

The aim was to understand the company’s growth, profitability, working-capital position, debt levels and the value suggested by different valuation approaches.
Key findings:
- Strong revenue growth: Revenue increased from ₹3,963 crore in FY20 to ₹9,973 crore in FY25, which is around 2.5 times growth. FY25 revenue increased by about 48% over FY24.
- Profit improved, but margins remain low: FY25 EBITDA was ₹763 crore and net profit was ₹251 crore. Net profit margin was about 2.5%, leaving a limited cushion if costs or interest expenses rise.
- Working-capital efficiency improved: The cash conversion cycle reduced from 44 days in FY21 to 8 days in FY25. Customer collection time also improved from around 116 days to 61 days.
- Debt is an area to watch: Debt-to-equity increased to 1.12x in FY25, while interest coverage was around 2.6x. This means higher borrowing costs could put pressure on earnings.
- The business needs significant investment: FY25 capital expenditure was about ₹556 crore. In the forecast, free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) remains negative from FY26 to FY30 because the model assumes capex stays high.
- Forecast shows continued expansion: Based on the model’s 25.06% annual revenue growth assumption, revenue is projected to reach ₹30,509 crore by FY30 and net profit ₹668 crore. These are model estimates, not guaranteed results.
Charts and what they show:
1. Historical performance
Revenue rose significantly over FY20–FY25, while net profit recovered to ₹251 crore in FY25. The chart also shows the dip in FY21 and the strong FY25 recovery.

2. Working-capital performance
The cash conversion cycle fell from 44 days in FY21 to 8 days in FY25, indicating faster conversion of working capital into cash.

3. Five-year forecast
The model projects revenue, EBITDA and net profit to increase each year through FY30. The forecast uses an annual revenue growth assumption of 25.06%.

4. Valuation comparison
After correcting the unit mismatch identified in the report, peer-based values are approximately ₹2,141–₹2,499 per share, compared with the market price of ₹7,770.50 used in the project (26 February 2026). The FCFF DCF output is negative because forecast free cash flow is negative in every forecast year.

Valuation takeaway
The peer-multiple comparison suggests that the market price was substantially higher than the values indicated by the selected peer multiples. However, this is only a reference point: Amber has a different business mix and growth profile, and a single-peer comparison may not capture that difference.

The report also identified issues that need to be fixed before relying on the valuation:
1. Some calculations mix ₹ lakh and ₹ crore, affecting outputs such as WACC and per-share value.
2. The WACC should be recalculated using consistent units and capital weights. The report estimates a corrected WACC of about 8.8%, rather than 6.48%.
3. The DCF terminal-year assumptions need review because capex remains above depreciation and forecast FCFF is negative throughout.
4. The dividend discount model is not suitable here because the company does not pay dividends; FCFE or residual income could be considered instead.
5. The valuation should be checked against a wider peer group.
